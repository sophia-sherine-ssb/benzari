Overview

This is the wood component of a dual-part project for EE 202L — Linear Circuits at USC. The course asked us to design and build a fully functional electric guitar from scratch, with a custom PCB housing a 196 Hz notch filter that suppresses the unfretted G-string fundamental.

Two intertwined halves: the woodwork (this page) and the electronics — filter design, PCB layout in KiCad, soldering, and assembly. This page covers the fabrication of the guitar body and neck in the USC Maker Space.

Two weeks. 23 pieces of recycled wood. 20 hours of fabrication. $45 in materials. One fully playable purple electric guitar.

System Architecture

The guitar is a complete electro-acoustic system. The pickup captures string vibration, feeds it through the notch filter PCB in the blue box, and outputs filtered audio through a jack to an external speaker.

Signal chain from string to speaker. The blue box — notch filter PCB, battery, LED, switch — lives inside the routed body cavity.

Wood Fabrication — The Body

No wood was purchased. The guitar body was built from 23 pieces of recycled wood — offcuts from other students' guitars in the Maker Space. Each piece was cut to even height and width, jointed on both faces to be perfectly flat, then glued and left overnight to cure.

A bandsaw cut the body shape, with grain running lengthwise for durability. A table router gave the edges their rounded profile. The shape was modeled after Prince's iconic guitar, chosen from the start.

Early stage — post-bandsaw. Blue rectangles mark cavity and box positions.

Post-table router — rounded edges visible. Body cavity for the blue box roughed out.

Wood Fabrication — The Neck

The neck was provided as raw stock. The headstock was bent and shaped, which ruled out piano pins — guitar tuning pegs were used instead. A channel was routed along the neck side to run the pickup cable into the blue box.

Fret slots were carved by hand using a saw and chisel. The walnut fretboard came pre-slotted. Fitting the neck into the body pocket required multiple rounds of test-fitting and adjustment.

Neck blank and walnut fretboard — checking fit before routing the cable channel.

Fret slots carved by hand, gold tuning pegs installed.

Details, Cracks & Repairs

Sanding revealed cracks not visible before. Each was sealed with wood glue and fine sawdust — pressed in, cured, sanded flush. The headstock was particularly affected and required careful taping and clamping. Multiple people in the Maker Space inspected and sanded the body; every pass revealed something new.

Headstock crack sealed with glue and wood dust, taped while curing.

Neck after final sanding — smooth profile, clean grain.

Painting — Purple & Gold

Modeled after Prince — purple and gold was the only choice. Krylon spray paint, multiple coats, left overnight between sessions. The paint ran out halfway and had to be replaced mid-session.

Every detail painted gold: frets, nut, screws, blue box top, back of neck. A real gold bridge with matching gold screws was fitted. Holes drilled for jack, LED, and switch. Finished weight: approximately 13 lbs.

First coat of Krylon purple — body shape and routed edges clearly visible.

Top view — pickup, blue box, and bridge installed. PCB and battery inside the routed cavity.

Back of body — deep purple finish, gold neck visible.

Completed guitar — purple body, gold hardware, walnut fretboard. The blue box on the right houses the notch filter PCB.

Reflections

This project was unexpected in the best way. It started as a circuits course and turned into a full woodworking and design challenge. Building something physical that works — that you can plug into a speaker and play — is a different kind of satisfaction than a simulation result on a screen.

Using recycled wood was a constraint that became a source of pride. The grain matching, the jointing, the hours of sanding — none of it was required, but all of it mattered. The more meaningful thing was learning that engineering and craft are not separate disciplines.

The electrical half — filter design, PCB layout in KiCad, soldering, and assembly — will be documented separately as the Electric Guitar (PCB with 196 Hz Notch Filter) project.
