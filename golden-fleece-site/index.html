25°C lower peak (simulated, approx.)

In our thermal simulation of an afternoon heat wave (air at 32°C, heat output rising from 3.5 kW to 10 kW), a bare transformer peaks at about 82°C and the same transformer with wax peaks at about 57°C. This is a simulation. We plan to validate it with a bench prototype.

Useful on an ordinary day. Still working in a crisis.

Ordinary day

The wax smooths the daily heat cycle, and the dashboard shows which units run hot, so crews inspect by exception instead of visiting every unit on a fixed schedule.

Crisis

During city-wide events and heat waves, the wax absorbs peak heat with no fans, no pumps and no staff on site.

Golden Fleece is not only for Los Angeles. Southern and western Europe are seeing heat-related outages on equipment built for milder weather, and the Gulf already runs its transformers below full load and oversizes their cooling for 50°C sites, where the wax would be an add-on. The idea is the same everywhere, with the wax's melting point matched to the local heat, since hotter nights call for a higher one. We are early. Our results come from a thermal simulation, the dashboard runs on synthetic data, and we have not built a physical prototype yet. Next, we plan a small bench test of the wax, then a first pilot with a utility.
