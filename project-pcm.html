The Problem

Los Angeles runs on a grid not designed for the heat it now faces. During summer heat waves, electrical transformers experience extreme thermal stress — as load increases and ambient temperatures rise, they overheat, derate, and in the worst cases, fail entirely.

During the 2006–2007 LA heat storms, more than 800 transformers failed, cutting power to tens of thousands of residents. The problem is compounding: heat waves are intensifying, energy demand is climbing, and the infrastructure is aging.

"More than 64,000 customers experienced power outages during a heat wave, as utilities worked on overheating equipment." — LAIST

The Approach

The project proposes a passive PCM-based cooling device — a unit filled with paraffin wax that attaches directly to a pad-mounted transformer. When the wax melts, it absorbs a large quantity of heat without itself rising significantly in temperature. Overnight, as ambient temperatures drop, the wax solidifies again, releasing stored heat and resetting for the next day.

No pumps. No electricity. No maintenance. Just thermodynamics. To validate the design, I built a thermal simulation in MATLAB and a 3D CAD model in Fusion 360.

MATLAB Simulation Code

Enthalpy-based lumped-parameter model with three local functions: simulatePCM, simulateNoPCM, and enthalpyToState.

Heat_Simulation.m  —  MATLABview only
%% pcm_transformer_demo.m
% Lumped-parameter simulation of transformer + PCM cooling
% Compares good PCM design vs no-PCM baseline

clear; clc; close all;

%% Time
dt   = 1;                     % time step [s]
tEnd = 8*3600;              % 8 hours [s]
t    = 0:dt:tEnd;
n    = numel(t);
th   = t/3600;                % time in hours for plotting

%% Heat input from transformer losses [W]
% Step-like peak load during hours 2 to 5
qGen = 3500 + 6500 * (t >= 2*3600 & t <= 5*3600);

%% Ambient temperature [degC]
% Hot afternoon first, then cooler evening
Tamb = 32 * ones(size(t));
Tamb(t >= 5*3600) = 24;

%% Common transformer properties
params.Ctr   = 500000;      % thermal capacitance of transformer [J/K]
params.Tinit = 32;          % initial transformer temperature [degC]

%% PCM properties
params.mPCM  = 180;         % PCM mass [kg]  (increased from 80)
params.cpPCM = 2200;        % PCM sensible heat capacity [J/(kg*K)]
params.L     = 180000;      % latent heat [J/kg]
params.Tm    = 55;          % melting temperature [degC]  (lowered from 82)
params.Tref  = 0;           % reference temperature [degC]

%% Good PCM design parameters
good.Rtp = 0.0005;          % transformer -> PCM thermal resistance [K/W]  (lowered from 0.002)
good.Rpa = 0.002;           % PCM -> ambient thermal resistance [K/W]  (lowered from 0.003)

%% No PCM baseline parameters
noPCM.Rta = 0.005;          % transformer -> ambient resistance [K/W]

%% Simulate
outGood = simulatePCM(t, dt, qGen, Tamb, params, good);
outNo   = simulateNoPCM(t, dt, qGen, Tamb, params, noPCM);

%% Figure 1: Temperatures
figure('Color','w');
plot(th, outNo.Ttr,    '--', 'LineWidth', 2, 'Color', [0.0 0.45 0.74]); hold on;
plot(th, outGood.Ttr,  '-',  'LineWidth', 2, 'Color', [0.85 0.33 0.10]);
plot(th, outGood.Tpcm, '-',  'LineWidth', 2, 'Color', [0.93 0.69 0.13]);
plot(th, Tamb,         ':',  'LineWidth', 2, 'Color', [0.49 0.18 0.56]);
grid on;
xlabel('Time [hours]'); ylabel('Temperature [degC]');
title('Transformer Cooling With PCM');
legend('No PCM transformer', 'Transformer with PCM', ...
       'PCM temperature', 'Ambient air', 'Location', 'northwest');

%% Figure 2: Heat flows and melt fraction
figure('Color','w');
subplot(2,1,1);
plot(th, qGen/1000,        'k',  'LineWidth', 2); hold on;
plot(th, outGood.qTP/1000, '-', 'LineWidth', 2);
plot(th, outGood.qPA/1000, '-', 'LineWidth', 2);
grid on;
xlabel('Time [hours]'); ylabel('Heat flow [kW]');
title('Heat Flow Paths — PCM Case');
legend('Transformer heat generation', 'Transformer -> PCM', ...
       'PCM -> Air', 'Location', 'northwest');

subplot(2,1,2);
plot(th, outGood.meltFrac, 'LineWidth', 2);
grid on;
xlabel('Time [hours]'); ylabel('Melt fraction');
ylim([0 1.05]);
title('PCM Melt Fraction (0 = solid, 1 = fully melted)');

%% ---- Local functions ----
function out = simulatePCM(t, dt, qGen, Tamb, p, d)
    n = numel(t);
    Ttr  = zeros(1,n);  Tpcm = zeros(1,n);
    x    = zeros(1,n);  qTP  = zeros(1,n);  qPA = zeros(1,n);
    Ttr(1) = p.Tinit;
    Hpcm = p.mPCM * p.cpPCM * (p.Tinit - p.Tref);
    [Tpcm(1), x(1)] = enthalpyToState(Hpcm, p);
    for k = 2:n
        qTP(k-1) = (Ttr(k-1) - Tpcm(k-1)) / d.Rtp;
        qPA(k-1) = (Tpcm(k-1) - Tamb(k-1)) / d.Rpa;
        dTtr = (qGen(k-1) - qTP(k-1)) * dt / p.Ctr;
        Ttr(k) = Ttr(k-1) + dTtr;
        Hpcm = Hpcm + (qTP(k-1) - qPA(k-1)) * dt;
        [Tpcm(k), x(k)] = enthalpyToState(Hpcm, p);
    end
    qTP(end) = (Ttr(end) - Tpcm(end)) / d.Rtp;
    qPA(end) = (Tpcm(end) - Tamb(end)) / d.Rpa;
    out.Ttr = Ttr; out.Tpcm = Tpcm;
    out.meltFrac = x; out.qTP = qTP; out.qPA = qPA;
end

function out = simulateNoPCM(t, dt, qGen, Tamb, p, n0)
    n = numel(t);
    Ttr = zeros(1,n);  Ttr(1) = p.Tinit;
    for k = 2:n
        qToAir = (Ttr(k-1) - Tamb(k-1)) / n0.Rta;
        dT = (qGen(k-1) - qToAir) * dt / p.Ctr;
        Ttr(k) = Ttr(k-1) + dT;
    end
    out.Ttr = Ttr;
end

function [T, x] = enthalpyToState(H, p)
    % Convert PCM enthalpy H into temperature T and melt fraction x
    HsolidEnd = p.mPCM * p.cpPCM * (p.Tm - p.Tref);
    HliqEnd   = HsolidEnd + p.mPCM * p.L;
    if H < HsolidEnd
        T = p.Tref + H / (p.mPCM * p.cpPCM);  x = 0;
    elseif H <= HliqEnd
        T = p.Tm;  x = (H - HsolidEnd) / (p.mPCM * p.L);
    else
        T = p.Tm + (H - HliqEnd) / (p.mPCM * p.cpPCM);  x = 1;
    end
    x = max(0, min(1, x));
end

Reflections

The most striking takeaway was how much cooling capacity can be unlocked from a material's phase transition alone. The latent heat of paraffin wax — 180,000 J/kg — is roughly fifty times larger than its sensible heat capacity over a 10°C range.

Simulation also clarified how important partial melting is as a design goal. The right combination of mass, melting point, and thermal resistance keeps the PCM actively absorbing heat for the entire high-load window. This project was developed alongside Lehar Goenka.

From the deserts of Morocco to the palm trees of Los Angeles, I work at the intersection of design, technology, and economics. After receiving my French Baccalaureate from Lycée Descartes in Rabat, I went on to study Biomedical Engineering at USC as part of the Class of 2028.

From oil painting in my backyard to generating CAD from hand sketches, I've explored as many scientific and artistic tools as I could from a young age. Over time, I've grown to value the process of designing and building technology that can meaningfully shape the world around us.

This space brings together projects I've worked on, along with what I've learned through them. For more, see the /about section for my resume and contact information.
